The idea that "Simple Christianity" is what is **sufficient** to understanding the theology of the Gospel. In some level it's true. The Gospel is simple in that it's the "... the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes" ([[Rom 1_16]]), and to whichever degree your theology defines belief as, that is the "Simple Christianity".

However, in the pursuit of maturing in our faith, perhaps it's inaccurate to believe that there's all there is to our faith.

Random reflections that caught my mind as I was reading [[1 Cor 6]]:
The idea that Christians are called to eventually judge angels in v2-3 *"Or do you not know that the saints will judge the world? And if the world is to be judged by you, are you incompetent to try trivial cases? 3 Do you not know that we are to judge angels?"*. 

This gives the principle that we should have a **desire to move towards wisdom** and discernment in all things because we understand that:
1. Things and Life are not always so black and white, there're grey situations that requires the derivation of higher-order biblical truths.
2. Often times we have incomplete information on the situation and people's heart, yet we have to make a judgment call on what's the maximally godly thing to do, balancing truth, love, grace, holiness, justice, kindness, patience, goodness, etc... the list goes on and on.

Therefore, this would render "Simple Christianity" harder to stand by, as to judge biblically requires "Deep Christianity" that ordinarily requires an emphasis on faithfully pursuing truth and not resting on the usual statements believers make to defend their doctrine: "The Bible says this means this" (Which is true, but it must also be interpreted in light of the rest of Scripture which may give new insight into the original meaning of the verse).

I say this because in [[COC Theology]], there's a tendency to quote bible verses and "let Scripture speak for itself". Which seems to make sense, but it fails to recognise the depth and context of every other single verse in Scripture, which when understood, will reframe how we view those original verses. Particular on the theology of [[Baptism]], [[Women's Prayer]], or [[Musical Instrument]], which I'm more familiar with.
