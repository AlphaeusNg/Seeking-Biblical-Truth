### **Why It’s Not Biblical to Condemn Musical Instruments in Worship**

The Church of Christ’s stance against musical instruments in worship primarily stems from the belief that the New Testament (NT) does not explicitly authorize them, based on the regulative principle of worship (i.e., "if it's not commanded, it's forbidden"). However, a thorough biblical examination reveals that rejecting musical instruments is not a biblically required position. Instead, Scripture supports their use as a legitimate and God-glorifying expression of worship.

---

## **1. The Old Testament Endorses Musical Instruments in Worship**

The most direct biblical evidence for musical instruments in worship comes from the Old Testament (OT), where God explicitly commanded and endorsed their use in temple worship.

### **A. Instruments Were Divinely Ordained for Worship**

1. **King David and the Temple Musicians**
    
    - _2 Chronicles 29:25_ – _"And he stationed the Levites in the house of the LORD with cymbals, harps, and lyres, according to the commandment of David and of Gad the king’s seer and of Nathan the prophet, for the commandment was from the LORD through his prophets."_
    - The use of instruments was not merely David’s personal preference—it was commanded by God.
2. **Instruments in the Psalms**
    
    - _Psalm 150:3-5_ – _"Praise him with trumpet sound; praise him with lute and harp! Praise him with tambourine and dance; praise him with strings and pipe! Praise him with sounding cymbals; praise him with loud clashing cymbals!"_
    - This is an open command for worshippers to praise God with instruments.
    - Nowhere in the NT does God revoke this form of worship.
3. **Instruments in Prophetic Worship**
    
    - _1 Samuel 10:5_ – _"After that you shall come to Gibeath-elohim, where there is a garrison of the Philistines. And there, as soon as you come to the city, you will meet a group of prophets coming down from the high place with harp, tambourine, flute, and lyre before them, prophesying."_
    - Even the prophets were accompanied by instruments as they prophesied.

### **B. The New Covenant Does Not Repeal the Old Testament’s Teaching on Instruments**

1. **Continuity Between Old and New Covenants**
    
    - Jesus said in _Matthew 5:17_ – _"Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them."_
    - While the sacrificial system was fulfilled in Christ, there is no indication that musical praise was abolished.
2. **Selective Nullification Is Inconsistent**
    
    - Many COC members accept singing from the Psalms (_Ephesians 5:19_, _Colossians 3:16_), yet they reject instruments that are repeatedly endorsed in those very Psalms.
	    - *19 speaking to one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord,*
	    - *16 Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom, teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord.*
    - If Psalm 150’s command to "sing" applies to us, why doesn’t the command to "praise Him with instruments"?

## **2. The New Testament Never Forbids Instruments**

A key argument from the COC is that the NT does not explicitly authorize instruments. However, this argument fails for several reasons:

### **A. The Absence of an Explicit Command Does Not Equal Prohibition**

1. **The NT Does Not Mention Many Common Church Practices**
    - The NT does not explicitly command:
        - Church buildings
        - Pews
        - Songbooks
        - Microphones
    - Yet, these are all permitted as expedient aids in worship.
      
2. **Jesus and the Apostles Never Condemn Instruments**
    - If musical instruments were truly sinful, we would expect clear NT prohibitions—yet there are none.
### **B. Early Christian Worship Likely Included Instruments**

1. **Revelation Portrays Heavenly Worship with Instruments**
    - _Revelation 5:8_ – _"And when he had taken the scroll, the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb, each holding a harp, and golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints."_
    - _Revelation 14:2_ – _"And I heard a voice from heaven like the roar of many waters and like the sound of loud thunder. The voice I heard was like the sound of harpists playing on their harps."_
    - If heaven’s worship includes instruments, why would the church on earth be prohibited from using them?
      
2. **The Greek Word for “Make Melody” in Ephesians 5:19 Implies Instruments**
    - _Ephesians 5:19_ – _"Addressing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody to the Lord with your heart."_
    - The Greek word for "making melody" is _psallō_, which historically meant "to pluck or play a stringed instrument."
    - If Paul had meant to forbid instruments, he would have used a different verb.

## **3. The Regulative Principle Is an Unbiblical Restriction**

The COC position assumes the _regulative principle_—that whatever is not commanded is forbidden. However, this principle is not biblically mandated.

1. **The Bible Permits More Than It Commands**
    
    - Colossians 3:17 says, _"Whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus."_
    - This allows for practices not explicitly mentioned, so long as they are done for God’s glory.
2. **Jesus Condemns Legalistic Worship Restrictions**
    
    - _Matthew 15:9_ – _"In vain do they worship me, teaching as doctrines the commandments of men."_
    - Imposing a man-made ban on instruments goes beyond biblical teaching.

---

## **Conclusion: Instruments Are Biblically Permissible and Can Enhance Worship**

- The Old Testament **explicitly commands and celebrates** instrumental worship.
- The New Testament **does not revoke or prohibit** instruments.
- The regulative principle is **not a biblical rule**, and adding restrictions where Scripture does not is legalistic.
- The Book of Revelation presents instruments as **part of heavenly worship**, suggesting their continued use in glorifying God.

Thus, to condemn musical instruments in worship is **not a biblical stance** but rather a human tradition. Instead of focusing on man-made prohibitions, churches should seek to worship God in spirit and truth (_John 4:24_), using every God-given gift to glorify Him—including music.
