My reflection when Phoebe asked me about this passage:

**To start, John 3:5 is a complicated passage, and we have to approach it carefully with lots of humility and deep reverence for Scripture’s authority! It’s complicated because there are several strong viewpoints, each with varying strengths and weaknesses. In my reflection, I’ll share my understanding: to be “born of water and the Spirit” in John 3:5 is to believe in Jesus and receive the Holy Spirit, not necessarily to be baptized with water. I’ll explain why, using Scripture, and address other views like baptismal regeneration. Let’s dive in.**

## **1. The Theme of Believing in Jesus**

As I read through John, I notice that the theme of believing in Jesus is emphasized over and over again (John 1:12, 50; 2:11, 22; 3:15–16, 18, 36). In John 1:12, it says that to those who believe in His name, Jesus _gives_ the right to become children of God, and in v13, to be **born** nor of flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God -> i.e. To be born of God is to believe in Jesus! **This sets the stage for understanding what it means to be “born again.”**

## **2. Two Baptisms: Water and the Holy Spirit**

We see John the Baptizer baptizing with water for repentance, preparing the way. But there’s another baptism Jesus will do, which seems like the “better baptism”—to be baptized by the _Holy Spirit_ (immersed, poured out). **This distinction is key because it shows that while water baptism is important, the Spirit’s work is central to rebirth.**

## **3. Old Testament Connections**

**This reminds me of** Ezekiel 36:25–27 and Isaiah 44:2–3, where God promises to pour or sprinkle His Spirit on His children. **These passages link water and the Spirit to cleansing and renewal.** Nicodemus, being a teacher of the law, would’ve known these verses well! **So, when Jesus speaks of being “born of water and the Spirit,” He’s likely drawing on these promises that Nicodemus should recognize.**

## **4. Jesus’ Challenge to Nicodemus**

**Building on this,** Jesus challenges Nicodemus’ understanding in John 3:3 and 3:10, asking what it truly means to be “born again.” He answers while staying consistent with God’s Word from the past. In John 3:15, 16, 18, and 36, it’s clear: whoever believes in Jesus shall not perish but have everlasting life. **So, to be “born again of water and the Spirit” is tied to believing in Jesus.**

## **5. Born of God, Not of Flesh**

John 1:13 and John 3:6 repeat the idea that those who believe in Jesus are born not of flesh, but of God. To be born of God is to be baptized with the Holy Spirit, and this must involve cleansing because God cannot dwell in sin. **That’s why Ezekiel and Isaiah make sense here—they promise both cleansing and the Spirit.**

**So, to be “born of water and the Spirit” means being baptized with the Holy Spirit through believing in Jesus, as John keeps emphasizing.**

## **Other Views: Baptismal Regeneration**

Could baptism be necessary to be “born again of water and the Spirit”? It could be, as many early church fathers supported baptismal regeneration (BR). **In fact, passages like Acts 2:38 and Mark 16:16 seem to link baptism with salvation, and early Christians often saw baptism as the means of regeneration.** This is strong evidence for BR, but I believe Scripture should take supreme authority over tradition.

**Here’s why I differ:** The rest of the Bible shows we are saved by grace through faith (alone), not by works, lest we boast (Ephesians 2:8–9). Baptism in Acts and elsewhere is a powerful symbol of repentance and proclamation of Christ, but justification comes from faith in Jesus (alone). For example, the thief on the cross was saved without baptism (Luke 23:40–43), and Romans 4 teaches that faith, not works like circumcision, justifies us. **Similarly, baptism is a _necessary_ picture of our faith, just as circumcision was for Israel, but it’s not what saves us.**

**So, passages like John 3:5, Acts 2:38, and Mark 16:16 must be understood in light of the whole Bible, which emphasizes faith for salvation.**

## **Conclusion**

**In humility, I see John 3:5 as spiritual rebirth through faith in Jesus, receiving the Holy Spirit (John 1:12, Ezekiel 36). Baptism pictures this, but faith saves.** We are baptized today in obedience to Jesus’ command (Matthew 28:19), not for salvation (Colossians 3, Romans 4), but as a symbol of being in Christ’s saving work (Galatians 3:26–27, Colossians 2:11–13, Romans 6:4).

**Once again, I recognize that I’m a finite sinner, and there could be inaccuracies! But this view makes sense to me, and I’m convicted of it. I’m open to probing questions as I continue learning!**
