### Confirmation Bias
How to fight against it:
First, seek out the other side. It’s really hard to do, and takes time, but find and read sources that challenge your thinking. Look for quality information, though—seeking out a different opinion is only useful if that opinion is credible and grounded in reality. This strategy is especially useful for writing or research projects. What you learn about opposing opinions will strengthen and refine your own opinion, which makes your writing stronger and more convincing.  

Second, pop your filter bubble. Social media feeds are confirmation bias (and fake news) gold mines! Your feeds are full of things you already agree with, but that doesn’t mean they’re accurate, true, or the whole story. Before you like or share something, do some lateral reading. To learn how, check out the Niche Academy Lateral Reading tutorial. Make this a habit, especially if you agree with what you’re reading. 

Finally, debate with yourself. Before continuing that argument with your friend, argue with yourself about whether plants have feelings. This will improve your research and writing by exploring arguments and counter-arguments before broadcasting them to the world. Think about what you believe, why you believe it, and if there is any room for additional information. You don’t have to convince yourself, but you’re less likely to fall into confirmation bias thinking if you take time to question what you don’t know, and—more importantly—what you do know.

### How to fight strawman arguments
- Restate their original clearly and accurately
- Make sure others agree with the point
- Acknowledge the good points they make
- Focus on the main idea

### How to be productive and not strawman people
- Get as much information about a topic as you can
- Learn about opposing ideas from people who hold them
- Focus on finding the strongest part of an opposing idea!
- Respect the opposing idea and the people who hold it
